1. BRAND STORY & MANIFESTO

1. BRAND STORY & MANIFESTO

1.1 The Origin Story

Think & Tinker was born from a deeply personal conviction: that every child deserves more than rote learning. Founded by Janeth Joseph, an educator with years of frontline experience in Lagos classrooms, Think & Tinker emerged from the gap between what children need and what the traditional system offers.

Janeth saw children who were brilliant but bored, curious but constrained, creative but confined to textbooks that never asked them to wonder.

She started small. A spiral-bound consult booklet for a single subject. A home lesson for one child who needed extra care. A Saturday morning where kids could read, build, paint, and ask questions without being told to sit still.

What began as a side passion alongside her teaching career grew into something the community began to rely on. Parents referred other parents. Schools asked for curriculum guidance. Teachers sought her out for training.

Today, Think & Tinker operates across five interconnected pillars, each designed to nurture the whole child. From its base in Gbagada, Lagos, the brand serves children aged 1 to 14 and their families, offering a complete educational support ecosystem that bridges what happens in school with what should happen everywhere else.

1.2 The Brand Manifesto

“Every child is a thinker. Every child is a tinker. Every child can shine.”

We believe learning should feel like play, and play should teach you something. We believe that a child who reads for joy will read for life. That a child who builds with their hands will one day build the future. That the best education does not happen in silence but in the mess of curiosity, in the noise of discovery, in the wonder of getting it wrong and trying again.

We exist to give every child in Lagos and beyond the tools, the confidence, and the space to think critically, create boldly, and explore fearlessly. We are not just a tutoring service. We are not just a bookstore. We are not just a Saturday club. We are a movement that places the child at the centre of their own learning journey.

We partner with parents who want more for their children. We equip teachers who want to teach better. We advise schools that want to be more than exam factories. And we build resources that make learning personal, practical, and permanent.

This is Think & Tinker. Where curiosity is the curriculum.

2. BRAND VOICE & COPYWRITING

2.1 Voice Personality

Think & Tinker speaks with the warmth of a favourite teacher and the confidence of an educational authority. The brand voice is simultaneously nurturing and knowledgeable, playful and professional. It never talks down to parents or children, but it always makes complex educational concepts feel approachable and actionable.

The Four Dimensions

Dimension Application
WARM We speak like a trusted family friend, not a corporate entity. Every message should feel like it comes from someone who genuinely cares about the child.
KNOWLEDGEABLE We demonstrate expertise without arrogance. We cite pedagogy when relevant, reference outcomes, and position ourselves as authorities parents can rely on.
PLAYFUL We embrace the energy of childhood. Our language is vibrant, sometimes cheeky, always optimistic. We use metaphors of building, exploring, and discovering.
EMPOWERING We give parents and children agency. Our language focuses on what the child can become, not what they lack. We celebrate progress, not perfection.

2.2 Tone Variations by Audience

Audience Tone & Objective Example Application
Parents & Guardians Tone: Reassuring, professional, partnership-oriented.

Parents are making an investment in their child. They need to feel confident. Use language that acknowledges their role as the primary decision-maker and positions us as their expert partner. 		"Your child’s potential is already there. We simply build the bridge between where they are and where they’re going. With personalised session notes, transparent progress tracking, and a dedicated educator who knows your child by name, you’ll never wonder what’s happening in a Think & Tinker session."
Teachers Tone: Collegial, practical, respectful.

Teachers are peers. Avoid any language that implies they are inadequate. Position Think & Tinker as a resource that makes their work easier and more effective, not a replacement. 		"We know the reality of a Lagos classroom. Large class sizes, limited resources, pressure to deliver results. Our consulting and curriculum tools are built by educators who have been in your shoes, designed to give you practical frameworks you can implement on Monday morning."
Schools Tone: Strategic, results-oriented, collaborative.

Schools think in terms of institutional outcomes, reputation, and compliance. Use language that speaks to measurable improvement and competitive advantage. 		"A well-structured curriculum does not just improve test scores. It transforms your school’s reputation, reduces teacher turnover, and creates a learning culture that parents actively seek out. Think & Tinker’s consulting arm helps you build that from the ground up."
Children Tone: Exciting, inclusive, encouraging.

Children should feel like Think & Tinker is their favourite place. Use short sentences, action verbs, vivid imagery, and inclusive language. 		"Ready to build something amazing? Grab your thinking cap and your tinker tools. Today, you’re the inventor, the storyteller, the explorer. Let’s go!"

2.4 Copywriting Do’s and Don’ts

DO DON'T
Use active, empowering verbs: build, explore, discover, create, shine Use passive or deficit language: struggling, failing, behind, weak
Centre the child as the hero of their story Position Think & Tinker as a rescue service
Acknowledge the parent’s role and intelligence Talk down to parents or imply they’re inadequate
Be specific about outcomes and processes Make vague promises: “we’ll change your child’s life”
Use Nigerian English naturally (e.g. “mummy”, “term”) Use American slang or culturally foreign references
Celebrate small wins and incremental progress Overpromise or guarantee specific grades/results

3. PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE

3. PRODUCT & SERVICE ARCHITECTURE

Think & Tinker’s ecosystem is organised into five interconnected pillars. Each pillar serves a distinct purpose but shares the brand’s common mission. Together, they form a complete educational support system that no single competitor in the Lagos market currently offers under one roof.

3.1 Pillar One — Consult Booklets

What It Is: Spiral-bound, subject-specific academic workbooks authored by Janeth Joseph. Each booklet is a self-contained learning companion that walks a child through an entire subject’s curriculum for their grade level, combining scheme-of-work mapping with structured question papers.

Structure of Each Booklet:

  • Cover Page: Subject name, grade level, Think & Tinker branding.
  • Purpose Page: Why the child needs this consult booklet.
  • Scheme of Work: Complete topic list aligned to the curriculum.
  • Author Information: Janeth Joseph’s credentials.
  • Introduction: How to use the booklet effectively.
  • Question Papers: Topic-by-topic exercises.

Target Audience: Parents seeking at-home support; tutors needing resources; schools.

Revenue Model: Direct sale. Can be bundled with tutorial packages.

3.2 Pillar Two — Tutorials (Home Lessons)

What It Is: Personalised, one-on-one or small-group tutoring sessions delivered at the child’s home or online. Tailored to specific needs, learning pace, and curriculum.

Service Flow:

  1. Initial consultation to assess needs and expectations.
  2. Parent completes admission form and signs contract.
  3. Invoice generation and payment processing.
  4. Scheduled sessions with post-session voice-transcribed notes uploaded to portal.
  5. Continuous progress tracking visible to parents via dashboard.

Differentiator: Session documentation via voice recording and transcription. This level of transparency is unmatched in the Lagos tutoring market.

Provides classical, editorial counterpoint. Used for quotations, testimonials, and formal documents (contracts, invoices).

6. LOGO SYSTEM

6. LOGO SYSTEM

6.1 The Combination Mark

The logo is a highly structured combination mark featuring a central symbol (the Secure Idea Cluster) positioned above a multi-tiered typography layout.

Symbol Functional Zones:

  • Left Hemisphere (Teal Gear): Represents STEM, engineering, and critical thinking mechanics.
  • Right Hemisphere (Orange & Gold Book): Represents literacy, reading, creativity.
  • The Core (Navy Shield & Keyhole): Anchoring the center is a deep navy shield with a keyhole. Visually communicates a zero-trust security vibe—assuring parents of safety and structure—while unlocking potential.
  • The Crown (Gold Rays): Five rays representing the "shine" and energy of discovery.

Typography Hierarchy:

  • Primary Wordmark: "THINK" in Tinker Teal, "&" in Deep Navy, "TINKER" in Spark Orange.
  • Operational Tagline: "LEARN. BUILD. SECURE. LEAD." framed by horizontal dividers.
  • Sector Badge: Solid Deep Navy pill badge containing "EDUCATIONAL FRANCHISE" in white, establishing corporate scale and authority.

6.2 Logo Variants

Full Logo (Stacked)The complete Idea Cluster above wordmark, tagline, and sector badge. For letterheads, hero headers, architectural signage.
Compact Logo"THINK & TINKER" text without tagline/badge. Used when vertical space is constrained.
Icon OnlyThe Secure Lightbulb only. Favicons, app icons, UI loading states.
TTKC WatermarkFaded, low-opacity version of the central shield/keyhole for official docs.

6.3 Spatial Governance

Clear Space: Minimum unobstructed area equals the height of the Deep Navy "&" symbol (1X).
Minimum Size: Full stacked logo never smaller than 40mm wide (print) or 150px (screen).
Misuse: Strictly prohibited: stretching, altering gear/book/shield arrangement, unauthorized colors, drop shadows, removing the badge in formal contexts.

9 & 10. VISUAL DIRECTION & APP

9. PHOTOGRAPHY & VISUAL DIRECTION

Photography builds trust. Parents need to see real children in real learning environments.

  • Shoot at child level: Camera at eye height. Communicates respect.
  • Capture action, not poses: Children reading, building, mixing. No lined-up group shots.
  • Show the environment: Colourful, messy, creative space. STEM equipment visible.
  • Diverse representation: Reflect Lagos’s diversity across ethnicity and ability.
  • Natural lighting: Avoid harsh flash. Warmth via golden hour or well-lit rooms.

Video Direction: Short-form (15-60s) for individual moments (mystery basket reveal). Long-form (2-5m) for recaps, parent testimonials, and Janeth's educational tips.
